You don't need to write PHP to make Monica better.

A clear bug report can save hours. A translation can make the product usable for someone who would otherwise struggle with it. An explanation of a confusing workflow can reveal a problem we stopped noticing months ago.

Those are contributions, even if GitHub doesn't give them all a nice green square.

### Start where the project is

For code, look at the current repository's contribution instructions before starting. They are a better source for setup details than an article that may outlive three development environments.

For a large feature, discuss the problem first. [Our prioritization article](/en/handbook/prioritize/) explains why a working implementation isn't automatically something we can maintain. Finding that out before spending two weekends on it is preferable for everyone.

Documentation and translations deserve the same care as code. Check what the interface actually does. Preserve meaning. If a sentence feels wrong, explain why rather than silently turning it into a promise we can't keep.

You can also help by showing someone how Monica works, sharing thoughtful feedback, or paying for the hosted service if it's useful to you. Financial support matters, but it doesn't buy control over the product's direction.

And please remove personal information from examples. A useful reproduction needs the shape of the problem, not your cousin's medical history.

Contribute something manageable, explain it clearly, and give people time to respond. That's a very good start.
