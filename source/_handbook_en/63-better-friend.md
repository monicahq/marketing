No piece of software can certify that you're a good friend. I wouldn't trust one that tried.

Monica can help you remember that someone was nervous about an appointment, that their dog died, or that you wanted to ask how a move went. Remembering those things can make a conversation more thoughtful.

But the thoughtful part still comes from you.

### A useful distinction

Writing “ask about the move” doesn't mean you've listened to the answer. A complete contact page doesn't mean you've been present when someone needed you. A reminder can bring an intention back to your attention; it can't supply the intention itself.

Likewise, forgetting a detail doesn't prove you don't care. People are tired, distracted, overwhelmed, and dealing with their own lives. We shouldn't make Monica another source of evidence against them.

I'd rather think of it as support for a limitation than an assessment of character. Glasses help people see. Nobody expects wearing them to make someone more observant about another person's feelings.

Some people will find that keeping notes helps. Others will find it unnatural or unnecessary. Neither choice establishes who cares more.

[We don't want to automate friendships](/en/handbook/automating-friendships/), and we don't want to grade them either.

If Monica helps you arrive at a conversation with a little more context, good. What you do with that context is where the relationship actually happens.
