A detailed roadmap would be very easy to write. I'd list all the things I'd like to build, attach some optimistic dates, and briefly feel extremely organized.

Then a serious bug would appear. Or life would happen. Or we'd discover that the feature scheduled for June was a terrible idea.

Now we'd have two problems: the work, and the promise.

### Explain the direction

[Our prioritization article](/en/handbook/prioritize/) explains why we don't maintain a detailed public delivery schedule. Monica is built alongside the rest of our lives. Our available time and our understanding of the product both change.

That doesn't mean people should have to guess what we care about. We can explain the problems we're exploring, show work in progress, and discuss why an idea belongs in Monica.

But an open issue isn't a reservation in a development queue. An interesting discussion isn't a commitment. And a feature being possible doesn't mean it's coming next month.

Occasionally we may announce a specific release target. That deserves more weight than a speculative idea, and we should be clear when circumstances change. Rejecting a permanent roadmap doesn't excuse careless announcements.

For now, use Monica for what it can do today. If an unbuilt feature is essential to you, waiting for it without a commitment from us would be a frustrating way to choose software.

I'd rather give an honest answer than maintain an attractive spreadsheet of future apologies.
