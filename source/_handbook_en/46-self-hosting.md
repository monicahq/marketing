If you have the skills and desire to operate Monica yourself, I think that's great.

Yes, that might mean one fewer customer for our hosted service. No, I haven't accidentally revealed a flaw in the business plan.

The point of the hosted version is convenience for people who want us to operate it. It shouldn't require pretending that running your own software is a bad idea.

### Control comes with work

Self-hosting lets you make decisions about the environment holding your information. It also makes you responsible for that environment: updates, access, backups, recovery, and the ordinary work of keeping a service running.

Owning a server doesn't automatically make it secure. A neglected installation isn't safer merely because it belongs to you.

Before choosing this route, use the installation instructions for the version you intend to run, understand its requirements, and think about how you'd recover from a failure. Downloading the code is the easy part.

The [prioritization article](/en/handbook/prioritize/) already explains why I encourage this independence. It also helps explain our preference for software that remains practical to operate outside our own setup.

If you don't want that responsibility, paying for hosting is reasonable too. There is no prize for spending your Saturday fixing a server when you wanted to record a nice memory.

We want both choices to make sense on their own terms. You don't need to become our customer to be someone the project was built for.
