I'm happy when someone stars Monica on GitHub. It means they noticed the project and wanted to keep track of it or show appreciation. That's nice.

It doesn't tell us whether they installed it, use it, understand it, or intend to contribute. It definitely doesn't tell the hosting provider to waive the invoice.

A star is a signal of interest. We shouldn't ask it to do a different job.

### Visibility changes the workload

A popular repository can bring contributors, useful feedback, and people who become long-term users. It can also bring more requests than a small team can handle.

Those outcomes don't arrive in convenient proportions. Ten times the attention doesn't produce ten times the maintenance capacity.

Comparing projects by star count also ignores what they're trying to do. A developer tool and an application holding private family information have different audiences and different reasons for being used quietly.

For Monica, I'd rather know whether someone can find a memory they recorded years ago, whether contributors can understand the code, and whether maintaining the project remains sustainable.

Some of those questions are much harder to summarize in a badge.

We can celebrate attention without confusing it with [success](/en/handbook/success/). Stars make it easier for people to discover Monica. What happens after discovery is the more demanding part.

Still, if you've starred it: thank you. I'm not above enjoying a nice number. I just don't want to put it in charge.
