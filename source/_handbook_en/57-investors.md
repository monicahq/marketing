The [original money article](/en/handbook/money/) already contains my diplomatic assessment of venture capital. I won't repeat it here. The diplomacy was quite concentrated.

The more useful question is what outside investment would ask Monica to become.

An investor reasonably expects a return. That can bring pressure to grow faster, pursue a larger market, or prioritize opportunities that make financial sense but don't fit why we started the project.

### Independence is useful in ordinary decisions

We want to be able to encourage self-hosting even when it doesn't generate a subscription. We want to spend time on a difficult relationship model without proving that it unlocks an enormous market.

We also want the option to remain small. A sustainable project that serves a limited audience can be a successful outcome for us, even if it wouldn't suit an investment fund.

This isn't a claim about every investor's motives, or a detailed account of Monica's finances. It's about the incentives we prefer to work under.

Bootstrapping has costs too. Less money can mean slower progress, fewer people, and work we can't undertake. Independence doesn't turn those limitations into magic advantages.

Still, I would rather choose a scope we can sustain than accept money that requires us to pursue a different project under the same name.

[Our definition of success](/en/handbook/success/) is allowed to be smaller than someone else's expected return. That's one of the reasons to protect it.
