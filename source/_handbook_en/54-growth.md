A sudden flood of new users sounds like a nice problem. It can also mean more support, more infrastructure work, and less time to fix the thing those new users are asking about.

The graph goes up. The available evenings remain stubbornly the same size.

For Monica, growth has to be considered alongside the capacity to maintain the product.

### More isn't automatically healthier

We want people who would benefit from Monica to discover it. We want the hosted service to support the work. But acquisition by itself doesn't tell us whether the project is becoming more sustainable.

Are people finding what they expected? Are we attracting users whose needs fit the product? Can we handle the responsibilities that come with them? Does the work remain something we want to do?

A promotional tactic that brings a lot of poorly matched signups might look successful while creating disappointment on both sides.

[Staying small](/en/handbook/staying-small/) is about the shape of the team. Here the question is pace: how quickly can the project grow without making promises its maintainers can't honour?

Sometimes improving the experience for existing users is more valuable than finding new ones. Sometimes a clearer explanation of what Monica doesn't do saves everyone time.

I don't want us to reject opportunity out of habit. I also don't want a growth target to quietly become the person making all our decisions. Targets have surprisingly little sympathy for a busy weekend.
