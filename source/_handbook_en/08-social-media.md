I don't want social media to become another full-time job attached to a project I already build outside my full-time job.

That's a fairly important starting point.

For Monica, social platforms are a place to show what we're building, explain a decision, and let interested people discover the project. They aren't a requirement to have an opinion about everything happening on the internet.

### Post when there is something to say

A small improvement with a clear explanation can be worth sharing. So can a difficult product question or something we learned while building it.

Silence is also fine. We don't need to invent content because a calendar has an empty square. The [marketing article](/en/handbook/marketing/) explains why I'd rather talk about the product than produce material merely to stay visible.

We should keep substantial explanations somewhere we control, such as the blog or this handbook. A short post can point people there without becoming the only place a decision was documented.

### Keep private things private

Screenshots should use invented information. An amusing example isn't worth revealing someone's real notes or relationships. The same judgment applies when discussing support questions in public.

A social account also shouldn't imply an always-available support desk. [Support capacity](/en/handbook/support/) doesn't expand merely because a platform makes it easy to send another message.

I want people to recognize the humans behind Monica. I don't want us to become professional participants in an algorithm's daily feeding schedule. The pandas already have enough appetite for this project.
