I started Monica because I forgot things. Names, conversations, details somebody had shared a few months earlier.

I cared about the people involved. Unfortunately, caring didn't make their child's name reliably appear when I needed it.

That's the fairly unglamorous origin of this project.

### Memory isn't a filing system

We shouldn't pretend this handbook is a neuroscience textbook. I can't explain every reason people forget. I can describe the practical problem: something felt memorable when I heard it, and later it wasn't available when I wanted it.

Life provides plenty of competition for attention. Work, family, ordinary errands, and all the other things happening between one conversation and the next don't disappear because a detail matters.

Writing something down gives you another place to look. It doesn't make the record complete or infallible. You might misunderstand, omit context, or discover that what you recorded is no longer true.

That's why a note should remain something you can question and update, rather than an official statement about another person forever.

Monica is useful to me as external memory. It's not evidence that people who use it care more, or that people who don't need it are doing something wrong.

[Being a good friend](/en/handbook/better-friend/) is a different question. Remembering a name can make an interaction easier. Forgetting it occasionally shouldn't require a moral investigation. Sometimes it just requires asking again, politely, and accepting a little embarrassment.
