I like building software. I have considerably less enthusiasm for building an organization whose main activity is organizing itself.

A larger company comes with people to hire, meetings to attend, budgets to negotiate, and decisions about who is allowed to make decisions. These are real jobs, and some people enjoy them. I would generally rather fix a form.

Monica already has a purpose: helping people document their lives. Becoming a large employer isn't a necessary step towards doing that well.

### Small has its advantages

A small team can understand the whole product. We can explain a decision without asking three departments for permission. We can spend time on an awkward relationship problem because it matters, even if nobody has prepared a convincing revenue forecast for step-siblings.

There are disadvantages too. Less capacity. Fewer people available when something breaks. Features we can't reasonably maintain. Pretending otherwise would be silly.

The answer isn't to promise everything and hope future employees materialize. It's to keep the scope compatible with the people doing the work.

This isn't a vow to keep an exact headcount forever. Circumstances change. It's a preference for a project we can understand, afford, and enjoy running.

If Monica becomes more useful without becoming a much larger company, that seems like a pretty good deal. The org chart can survive the disappointment.
