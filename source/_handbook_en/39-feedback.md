“Please add this button” is a possible starting point. “I can't find the conversation I recorded before visiting my friend” tells us considerably more.

The second describes a problem. There might be several ways to solve it, including one that doesn't add another button to everyone's screen.

### Give us the situation

What were you trying to do? What happened instead? Was this a one-time problem or something you encounter regularly? Which version of Monica were you using, and was it hosted or self-hosted?

If it's a bug, the smallest set of steps that reproduces it is especially useful. An error message can help too, after checking it doesn't contain personal information or credentials.

Please use invented people in examples. We don't need access to your real records to understand that a relationship is displayed in the wrong direction.

Context also helps with feature requests. Explain the task before defending a particular implementation. [Our product philosophy](/en/handbook/product-philosophy/) starts with the problem for a reason.

We won't always agree with the proposed solution. Sometimes we won't have time to respond quickly. Neither means your experience is unimportant.

Be direct. You don't need to wrap criticism in three compliments. But remember that the person reading it is another human being, often after their day job. “This workflow is confusing” is useful. “Whoever built this is an idiot” mostly makes the evening worse.
