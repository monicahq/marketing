A comparison page would be easy to arrange. Put Monica in the first column, choose the features Monica has, and distribute green ticks generously.

Congratulations to us on winning the competition we designed ourselves.

I don't find that especially useful for someone choosing software.

### Explain our own decisions

Other products can be excellent without sharing our priorities. A tool built for sales teams, a private notebook, and a personal CRM are solving overlapping but different problems.

We should be able to explain why Monica is open source, why self-hosting matters, and why we care about documenting relationships. Those arguments don't require making someone else's product look foolish.

This doesn't mean refusing to notice good ideas elsewhere. [Our principles](/en/handbook/principles/) openly describe sources of inspiration. Learning from another team is healthy. Automatically copying every feature they release is a different activity.

It also doesn't prevent answering a specific question about differences. If someone needs a capability Monica doesn't have, we should say so accurately instead of changing the subject to our superior panda collection.

The danger is letting someone else's release schedule become our product strategy. A feature can be useful in their context and wrong in ours.

I'd rather explain Monica clearly enough for people to make their own comparison. They know what they need better than a table constructed to ensure that we win.
