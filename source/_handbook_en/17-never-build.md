A leaderboard of your best friends would be technically possible. So would a score showing which cousin offers the highest return on your emotional investment.

We should build neither. Ideally, we shouldn't have to hold a meeting about it.

Monica exists to document your life, not to rank the people in it. That rules out a whole category of features: relationship scores, competitive friendship streaks, and systems that treat a missed conversation as a personal failure.

### Useful to someone isn't enough

A public feed of your private notes might increase engagement. It would also fundamentally change what Monica is. We don't want to become a social network simply because we already have names and profile pictures in a database.

Likewise, identifying commercial opportunities in your friendships would push us towards the sales CRM mentality that Monica was created to get away from.

There are also perfectly reasonable features that belong in another product. A complete photo library or an elaborate sales pipeline might be excellent software. We don't have to build it here.

[Our scope](/en/handbook/where/) is the starting point. The test isn't whether an idea is impressive, popular, or easy to generate code for. It's whether it helps people document their lives without turning those lives into something to optimize.

Saying no to a feature leaves time for the product we've actually chosen to maintain. It's a decision, not a missing capability in our imagination.
