Representing a family properly is difficult. Remembering a birthday across time zones can be difficult. Choosing a technology stack shouldn't become a third research project unless it has to.

I prefer spending our limited time on the problems that make Monica useful. That's why [our principles](/en/handbook/principles/) favour technologies we can understand and maintain over architectural fashion.

“Boring” doesn't mean unsupported, obsolete, or immune to security problems. It means that other people have already encountered many of its problems, written explanations, and worked out reasonable solutions.

### Budget the surprises

An unfamiliar technology might be exactly what a problem needs. But then we should be able to explain what it makes better for Monica, including the cost of operating it and teaching it to future contributors.

“Everyone is talking about it” tells us almost nothing about either.

A self-hostable application also has to remain practical for people outside our development environment. A clever stack that only its author can install isn't much of a gift to the community.

I like learning new things. Monica is partly how I do that. But there is a difference between exploring a technology and making everyone's memories depend on my experiment.

The interesting part of Monica should be what it helps you remember. If the database stores a note correctly without generating a conference talk, I'm willing to live with that disappointment.
