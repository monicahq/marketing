I understand wanting help remembering to contact someone. I don't understand wanting to remove yourself from the conversation entirely.

If a system writes a caring message, sends it automatically, and handles the reply, at what point are you actually involved in the friendship?

There is a boundary between supporting attention and simulating it.

### Remove friction carefully

A reminder can help you remember your own intention. A note can help you recall what someone told you. Both leave the human decision and the human interaction with you.

Automatically performing affection is a different proposition. The apparent personal effort can become something the other person is responding to without knowing it wasn't yours.

Not every useful shortcut is a problem. Making information easier to find or helping someone communicate accessibly can support their participation. The question is whether the tool helps the person show up or quietly substitutes for them.

[Our relationship with AI](/en/handbook/ai/) uses the same distinction when considering ideas for Monica. Convenience alone isn't enough to justify a feature.

Relationships can involve effort, awkwardness, and conversations you don't know how to start. Removing all of that isn't necessarily an improvement. Sometimes the effort is part of what you wanted to give.

I want Monica to help you remember that a friend is having a difficult week. I don't want it to manufacture a friendship update while you do something else. They wanted to hear from you.
