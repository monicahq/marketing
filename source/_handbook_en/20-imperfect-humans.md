You know someone's birthday is in March. You don't know the day. You know they have a brother, but you can't remember his name. A relationship ended, then resumed, then became difficult to describe without a fairly long walk.

This is normal human information. A database might object. That's the database's problem.

Monica needs to be useful before someone has completed a perfect record of their entire social life.

### Unknown isn't the same as wrong

A blank field can mean “I don't know”, “I don't care to record this”, or “this doesn't apply”. We shouldn't pressure people into inventing an answer just to satisfy a form.

Names, families, and relationships also vary between cultures and households. Our own experience is a starting point for design, not a universal specification for being a person.

The [rebuild announcement](/en/blog/we-are-rebuilding-monica/) describes how thinking about relationships and customization changed after years of working on Monica. Good defaults matter, but a default shouldn't become an argument with someone's actual life.

There is a limit, of course. We can't anticipate every situation or turn every screen into a configuration panel. We can avoid making unnecessary assumptions, use neutral language, and listen when our model doesn't fit.

I'd rather Monica help someone keep one incomplete, meaningful note than make them feel they failed an administrative examination. Their family is already complicated enough. It doesn't need validation errors.
