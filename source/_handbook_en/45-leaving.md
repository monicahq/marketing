The longer you use Monica, the more valuable the information inside it can become. That creates a responsibility for us.

It would be easy to confuse “people have years of memories here” with “people can't reasonably leave”. Those should never become the same thing.

We want someone to stay because Monica remains useful, not because the exit is a technical hostage situation.

### Portability is a real problem

Moving personal information isn't just downloading names and email addresses. Relationships, notes, dates, and the context connecting them matter too. A file can exist and still be a poor representation of what someone wanted to keep.

That's why the quality and scope of exports deserve attention. The formats and transfer paths available depend on the version you're using; this article doesn't promise that every piece of data already transfers to every other product.

Before relying on a workflow, check what it includes and try opening the result. Having an export button and having a usable copy of your information are related but different things.

Self-hosting and public code provide additional options, though operating software takes work. [Leaving the hosted service](/en/handbook/self-hosting/) shouldn't require becoming a maintainer unless that's what you want.

We should judge future decisions partly by whether they preserve those options. A feature that makes Monica more useful while making your information harder to recover has a cost worth discussing.

A pleasant goodbye is a legitimate product goal. Unusual, apparently.
