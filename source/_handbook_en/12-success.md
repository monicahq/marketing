I could tell you that success means reaching a particular number of users. It would sound serious. I could put the number in a presentation and draw an arrow pointing upwards. People love an arrow.

But Monica started because I couldn't remember things about people I cared about. So the first useful measure is fairly obvious: does it help someone remember something they wanted to remember?

Someone opens Monica before visiting a friend, remembers what that friend was going through, and asks about it. Nothing particularly measurable happens inside the application. The useful part happens over dinner, where our analytics have absolutely no business being.

### Enough is a useful word

Money matters. A project that can't cover its costs has a problem, however lovely its intentions. But making enough to keep going and extracting the largest possible amount of money are different ambitions.

I also want to keep learning. I want to look at something we've built and feel proud of it. I want working on Monica to remain a reasonable way to spend an evening, rather than a punishment I invented for myself in 2017.

Those things belong in the definition of success too.

[GitHub stars](/en/handbook/github-stars/) are encouraging. Revenue is useful. Neither tells the whole story. A small project that helps people and remains enjoyable to maintain is a perfectly respectable outcome. We don't need to apologize for it.
