The title is deliberately blunt. It needs a little context before everyone gets annoyed.

If you use Monica, you should be able to report a problem, ask a question, and disagree with a decision. You shouldn't need to flatter the maintainers to be heard. In return, we should be honest about the product and respectful when we answer.

But an open source licence doesn't include unlimited access to the people who wrote the software.

### Freedoms and expectations

You can inspect the code, modify it, and use it within its licence. Those freedoms are substantial. They don't require us to implement your feature, review a large contribution immediately, or spend a family weekend debugging your particular server configuration.

A paid service has its own obligations. This article isn't a way to wave away responsibilities to customers. It's about the assumption that publishing code creates an unlimited personal debt to anyone who encounters it.

Monica is built around other jobs and ordinary lives. As [the introduction](/en/handbook/project/) explains, our available time is limited.

We can say no without thinking you're a bad person. You can choose another product without betraying the project. Nobody has to turn a disagreement about a contact form into a moral emergency.

Clear boundaries make it more likely that we'll still enjoy maintaining Monica later. That's useful to everyone, including people waiting for a reply.
