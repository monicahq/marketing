There are pandas in Monica because we like pandas.

I realize this explanation lacks a market segmentation study. Somehow we'll have to continue.

Software about your personal life doesn't have to look like an invoice management system. A little warmth can make an application feel as though actual people made it and cared about the details.

### Decoration can have a purpose

An illustration won't fix a confusing form. It can still make an empty page more welcoming or turn a dull corner of the site into something someone enjoys.

Not every worthwhile detail has to improve a metric. Sometimes we can make something because it's nice. [Our principles](/en/handbook/principles/) explicitly leave room for that.

There are limits, though. Monica can contain grief, illness, and difficult memories. A cheerful animal isn't the correct response to every situation. Neither is a joke when someone is trying to understand whether their information has been deleted.

The [writing article](/en/handbook/writing/) makes that distinction: personality belongs where it helps, and clarity comes first when the task is serious.

I want the project to feel playful without requiring users to enjoy our jokes in order to use it. That's especially important across languages, cultures, and moods.

The pandas aren't a strategy for increasing emotional brand attachment. They're pandas. If they make someone smile while visiting the handbook, they've done a respectable day's work. By panda standards, possibly an exhausting one.
