There are two ways of writing for Monica:

- the user interface in the product,
- the marketing material.

Both serve very different purposes and, as such, should have very different tones of voice.

### Writing for the product

For the user interface, the tone should be simple, direct, informative, and serious. Actions in the user interface should use the imperative form. For instance, we would say "Save" instead of "I save."

People don't come to Monica to read. They come to accomplish something. Every unnecessary word in the interface is potentially getting in the way of that.

We should use words that everyone understands. We shouldn't invent our own vocabulary when perfectly good words already exist. We shouldn't use technical terms just because they happen to be the terms we use in the code.

We should also be consistent. If we call something a contact in one part of the application, we shouldn't call it a person in another. Users shouldn't have to learn a new vocabulary every time they discover a feature.

Error messages should explain what happened and, whenever possible, how to fix it. Saying that something went wrong without providing any meaningful information is not particularly helpful.

We should also never blame the user. If someone consistently makes the same mistake in the application, there is a good chance that the application is the problem.

There is room for personality in the product, of course. A cute panda on an empty state or a funny message on a 404 page is perfectly fine. But humour should never come at the expense of clarity, especially when something serious happens.

### Writing for marketing

For the marketing material, we should be really approachable, funny, provide a lot of value, and not take ourselves seriously at all.

We should write as if we were talking to someone in real life. There is no reason to suddenly start using fancy words just because we're writing something that will appear on a marketing site.

We don't empower people. We don't unlock their potential. We don't provide a revolutionary, next-generation experience. We build software. Hopefully, useful software.

We can be opinionated. We can be sarcastic. We can make jokes that probably only three people will understand. We can acknowledge that some of the things we do are a bit ridiculous.

We are not a big corporation, and we shouldn't try to sound like one. One of the advantages of being a small project is that we can actually have a personality. Let's not waste that opportunity.

### Writing with empathy

Monica is a somewhat particular piece of software because of the nature of the information it contains.

People will document happy things in Monica. They will also document deaths, divorces, illnesses, difficult relationships, and some of the worst moments of their lives.

We should be mindful of that when we write things in the application. Not every interaction is a happy one. Not everything deserves a congratulatory message or a funny illustration.

We don't know what people are going through when they open Monica. We shouldn't make assumptions about how they feel, what their relationships look like, or what matters to them.

Sometimes, the best thing we can do is simply provide the functionality and say as little as possible.

### Writing for everyone

Monica is used by people from all over the world. For many of them, English is not their first language. It's not mine, either.

We should keep that in mind when we write. Short sentences, simple words, and straightforward explanations are generally better than complicated expressions that only native English speakers will understand.

This is particularly important in the product itself, where everything needs to be translated into many languages. A clever play on words in English might make absolutely no sense in Japanese.

We don't write to demonstrate how good we are at writing. We write to be understood.

### Be genuine

We should never say something we don't actually believe just because it sounds good.

If a feature has limitations, let's talk about them. If we made a mistake, let's acknowledge it. If we don't know the answer to something, let's say so.

We should avoid making promises about the future that we might not be able to keep. There is nothing wrong with saying that we'd like to do something without knowing whether we'll ever get around to doing it.

We don't have to pretend that everything we do is great.

### Remain humble

In all cases, though, we should remain extremely humble.

We know very little about how other people live their lives. Building a personal CRM doesn't suddenly make us experts in human relationships, psychology, or life in general.

We should never talk to our users as if we know better than they do. We shouldn't tell them how to be better friends, better parents, or better human beings.

We build tools that might help them along the way. That's all.

Monica exists for the sole purpose of helping others. The way we write should always reflect that.

We can be proud of what we build without thinking too highly of ourselves.
