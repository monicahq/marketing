Someone opens Monica, checks a detail, and leaves after thirty seconds.

Excellent. Go have the conversation.

There is no reason for us to keep that person around with an endless feed, a mysterious notification, or a streak they must protect. Monica isn't more useful because someone spent their whole lunch break looking at it.

### A reminder should earn the interruption

Reminders make sense in Monica because forgetting something is part of the problem we're trying to solve. That doesn't make every notification useful.

A birthday you chose to remember has a purpose. A message saying “We miss you!” mainly tells you that a piece of software has developed attachment issues.

We should ask what an interruption helps the person do, whether they asked for it, and whether it still makes sense in their circumstances. More notifications aren't automatically better support for relationships.

The same applies to the interface. There shouldn't be an obligation to clear every empty field or resolve a dashboard full of invented social debt.

Some people will use Monica frequently. Others will return when something happens or when they need a memory. Both are reasonable. As [our definition of success](/en/handbook/success/) explains, the useful result may happen entirely outside the application.

I want Monica to be easy to come back to. I don't want leaving it for the afternoon to require willpower.
