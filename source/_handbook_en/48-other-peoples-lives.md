The person typing a note isn't always the person the note describes.

That's one of the uncomfortable facts at the centre of Monica. A friend can tell you something in confidence without expecting it to become a permanent entry in a database.

Building a tool for personal memory doesn't make that tension disappear.

### Keep the purpose in view

Remembering that someone dislikes a particular food and keeping a secret history of their movements are very different activities. “It's for my personal records” doesn't make every use considerate.

Before recording something, ask why you need it, how sensitive it is, and whether you'd be comfortable explaining that decision to the person involved. The answer may be that you shouldn't keep it at all.

Context can also change. Something a friend said in anger isn't necessarily a permanent description of who they are. A difficult period shouldn't become their defining characteristic in your notes forever.

Sharing introduces another responsibility. Information appropriate for your own memory isn't automatically appropriate for another reader, even someone you trust.

This isn't legal advice or a universal rule for every situation. It's a request for judgment and restraint. Monica can't supply either merely by providing a text box.

[The question of creepiness](/en/handbook/creepy/) approaches this from the user's side. From the product side, we should remember that collecting more information isn't inherently a better outcome. Sometimes the most respectful record is the one you decide not to create.
