This project has one scope: it's about documenting your life and the information you know about the people you care about. That's the only scope.

This project is not meant to be:

- a social network,
- a repository for all your photos,
- a contact book storing all your contacts,
- a place where all the possible very specific use cases could live, like things that match your particular life and not the lives of many others.

A great project is the direct result of all the no's, not the yes's. For every yes, there should be a thousand no's.

We don't want Monica to become a piece of software where everything is configurable through an option. The more options there are, the harder it is to test the software with all the possible edge cases. Options are generally bad in consumer products. We'll introduce a new option only if we have no choice.

We welcome feature requests, but a feature being useful doesn't necessarily mean it belongs in Monica. There are plenty of great ideas that would make Monica a worse product if we implemented all of them.

Adding a feature is relatively easy. Maintaining it for the next ten years is a completely different story. Every feature we introduce is a commitment we make. This is especially true for an open source project maintained by a very small team.

We also shouldn't be afraid of removing things. If something doesn't make sense anymore, is too complicated, or doesn't bring enough value, we should be able to remove it.

We want Monica to be useful in 20 years. The information you put in Monica today should still be relevant decades from now. We don't want to build software around what's popular this year. We want to build software around things that fundamentally don't change: people, relationships, memories, and life itself.

We don't want Monica to be a product that you have to use every day. There is no reason for us to try to maximize the time you spend in the application. If you use Monica once a month and it brings you value, that's perfectly fine. You have a life to live outside of Monica.

We also don't want Monica to tell you how to live your life or how to manage your relationships. We provide tools. What you do with them is entirely up to you.

Your data is yours. It should always be possible to leave Monica without losing the information you've spent years documenting. We want you to stay because you like the product, not because it's too complicated to leave it.

We won't introduce AI just because everyone else is doing it. If AI can make something genuinely easier, we'll consider it. Technology should serve the purpose of the project, not define it.

We don't measure the success of Monica by the number of features it has, the number of times you open it, or the amount of data you put into it. If Monica helps you remember something important that you would otherwise have forgotten, it has done its job.

We don't want Monica to become the only piece of software you need in your life. We simply want it to be really good at the one thing it was designed to do.
