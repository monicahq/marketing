I don't have a verified, current breakdown to put in this article. Inventing one would make the page more visually satisfying and considerably less useful.

What we can explain is the shape of the costs.

### More than a database

The [money article](/en/handbook/money/) already mentions hosting and the services involved in sending reminders. Running a hosted application can also involve storage, recovery arrangements, monitoring, domains, and payment processing. The actual services and amounts depend on the setup; this isn't an assertion that Monica currently buys every item on that list.

A meaningful financial breakdown would need a period, a currency, the scope of the service being described, and a clear distinction between actual expenses and estimates.

Without those, a number can look transparent while answering very little.

Then there is time. Reviewing a bug, maintaining dependencies, answering questions, and operating the service all compete with building new things. That time is real even when nobody invoices the project for it.

This is why “the server is cheap” doesn't settle the question of whether a hosted service is sustainable. Software isn't a database we switch on and occasionally admire.

I'd welcome a properly documented cost breakdown when there's one to share. Until then, explaining the categories honestly is better than publishing a very precise guess.
