Paying for the hosted version should be a reasonably simple decision: is the convenience and usefulness worth the price to you?

We should make that question easier to answer, not surround it with confusing limits, unexplained fees, or language that changes meaning halfway through checkout.

The current pricing page is where actual offers and terms belong. This article is about how I think those decisions should be made, not a promise to keep a particular price forever.

### Fair has two sides

A price needs to respect the person paying. It also needs to support the cost of offering the service and maintaining the software.

Charging too little can look generous while leaving the project unable to sustain itself. Charging as much as possible because someone's memories are difficult to move isn't a model I want for Monica either.

[Self-hosting](/en/handbook/self-hosting/) gives people another option if they prefer to operate the software themselves. That doesn't make hosting valueless. It means we should be clear about what the payment is for.

Changes deserve understandable explanations. So do limitations. If someone needs a long conversation to work out what a plan includes, we've probably made the decision harder than necessary.

I like simple pricing because it reduces a particularly uninteresting form of administrative work. Nobody started looking for a place to remember their friends because they were hoping to negotiate a software contract.
