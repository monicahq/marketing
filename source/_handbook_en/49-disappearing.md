I'd like Monica to exist for a long time. I'd also like to avoid confusing that preference with a guarantee about the future.

Projects end. People change jobs. Hosting services close. Software built around personal memories should take that possibility seriously rather than hide it behind a cheerful mission statement.

### Different things can disappear

A hosted service can close while the software remains available. An open source repository can remain available while nobody is actively maintaining it. A copy of your data can survive while becoming difficult to use.

Those are different problems, and each needs more than the words “open source” as an answer.

Public code gives people the possibility of continuing the software or operating their own copy. It doesn't guarantee that someone will do that work, or that an old version will keep running unchanged on future systems.

[Usable exports](/en/handbook/leaving/) matter because preserving information shouldn't depend entirely on preserving the application. Keeping independent copies and understanding how to read them is more useful than assuming a familiar website will always be there.

We don't have a basis here to promise a particular shutdown notice period, escrow arrangement, or automatic transfer to another operator. Those would be specific commitments, not reassuring paragraphs.

The practical ambition is to make decisions that leave you options. A good personal memory tool should acknowledge that your life, and the value of what you recorded, can outlast the people who built it.
