Rejecting someone's work feels bad. They spent their time trying to improve Monica, and we'd generally prefer to say thank you and merge it.

But a pull request isn't just a gift of code. It's a proposal for the product we'll be responsible for afterwards.

Sometimes the code is good and the proposal still doesn't fit.

### Review the responsibility too

An integration might work beautifully today while leaving us responsible for an external service we don't want to follow for years. A new setting might solve one person's problem while making an already difficult screen harder for everyone else.

A feature can also conflict with [what Monica is for](/en/handbook/where/). No amount of elegant implementation makes a friendship leaderboard a good fit.

When possible, we should explain the reason for a rejection. “This doesn't belong in the product because…” is more useful than making the contributor guess whether their code, their idea, or their existence offended us.

Smaller contributions and early conversations reduce wasted effort. They don't guarantee acceptance. A discussion can expose complications none of us saw initially.

We should also be willing to say when the problem is our capacity. An indefinitely neglected pull request isn't necessarily kinder than an honest no.

[Contributing](/en/handbook/contributing/) should be worthwhile even when a particular change doesn't land. A good review can clarify the problem, suggest a smaller approach, or explain a boundary. It shouldn't become a competition over who cared more.
