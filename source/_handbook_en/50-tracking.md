You visit a website about remembering your friends. Later, the same product appears beside an article, a video, and a completely unrelated thing you were trying to buy.

It's an odd introduction for software asking to hold private information.

In [our marketing article](/en/handbook/marketing/), I explain that I don't like being tracked across the internet. I don't want Monica's promotion to depend on a practice I'd find unpleasant as a user.

### Measure a question, not a person’s entire day

Understanding whether people can find the product is reasonable. Following the same person across unrelated services to keep advertising at them is a different ambition.

Cookies also aren't all the same. Remembering a preference or maintaining a session is different from building an advertising profile. We should explain what a particular mechanism does rather than use “cookies” as a vague synonym for either harmless or evil.

The site's cookie notice says it doesn't use third-party cookies or ad trackers. That statement needs to remain consistent with the actual site, including scripts we add later. This article isn't an audit of every production system or a promise that no operational logs exist.

The principle is simple: collect what answers a legitimate, specific question, and don't quietly turn curiosity about Monica into permission to follow somebody around.

If someone leaves the site and thinks about something else, that's allowed. The panda doesn't need to accompany them to the weather forecast.
