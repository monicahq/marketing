Some people hear about Monica and immediately understand why they'd use it. Others look at it and think, “I could just use a notebook.”

Yes. You could.

If a notebook works well for you, we don't need to stage an intervention.

### Explain the fit honestly

[Who we build for](/en/handbook/audience/) guides product decisions. This article is about how we talk to people who might never become users.

We should show what Monica helps with: remembering details, preserving personal context, and finding things again. We should also make its limits visible. It won't do the work of caring about someone, and it isn't a sales platform with a friendlier colour palette.

Someone should be able to understand that and decide against it without having to dismiss three popups insisting they reconsider.

Marketing becomes unpleasant when it starts by manufacturing insecurity. You weren't worried about your friendships this morning, but apparently this website has discovered that you're failing at them. Conveniently, it also sells the cure.

We shouldn't do that.

There are enough people who already want a tool like Monica. Our job is to help them find it and judge whether it fits. That includes accepting that another product, a notebook, or no system at all might be better for them.

Losing an unsuitable customer before they pay is considerably nicer than acquiring an unhappy one. Less exciting for the conversion chart. Better for the humans involved.
