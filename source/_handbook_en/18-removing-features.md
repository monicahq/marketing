Adding a feature feels productive. Removing one can feel like admitting defeat. This is unfortunate, because occasionally the best thing we can do for Monica is take something out.

A field nobody understands still needs explaining. An obscure option still creates combinations we need to consider. A screen that made sense years ago can become an obstacle after the rest of the product changes.

The work doesn't disappear because we stopped being excited about it.

### Removal has a cost too

We shouldn't confuse our boredom with evidence that something is useless. A feature that looks unimportant to us might be where someone has documented years of their life.

Before removing something, we should understand the problem it solves, whether another part of Monica solves it better, and what happens to information people already entered. “The interface is cleaner now” isn't much comfort if your notes vanished.

Sometimes the right change is combining two concepts. Sometimes it's choosing a sensible default instead of preserving five switches. Sometimes it's leaving the feature alone because the disruption would cost more than the simplification is worth.

[Code is a liability](/en/handbook/code-liability/), but people's data isn't disposable clutter. Those two facts have to coexist.

I like the idea of a product getting easier to understand as it matures. Ideally, experience should produce fewer awkward decisions for the user. Otherwise we've simply spent ten years accumulating buttons.
