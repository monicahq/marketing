If someone wants to stop paying for Monica, making them frustrated won't make the product more useful.

It might produce another payment. It will also produce a perfectly reasonable dislike of us.

I don't consider that a good trade.

### Explain what happens

Cancellation should be understandable. People should know what happens to billing, access, and their information. Stopping a renewal, deleting an account, and exporting data are different actions; confusing them can have real consequences.

We should avoid guilt, artificial obstacles, and mandatory conversations whose only purpose is to delay a decision the person has already made.

An optional explanation of what didn't work can help us improve Monica. It shouldn't become a toll booth on the way out.

These are design principles, not instructions for a particular billing screen or a claim about refund terms. The exact process and consequences need to be checked in the service being used.

[Data portability](/en/handbook/leaving/) is part of the same responsibility, but it isn't the same task. A person might want to stop paying without erasing their history. We should make those distinctions clear before they act.

I'd like people to remember Monica as a useful thing they used for a while, even if it eventually stopped fitting their needs. We don't need to ruin that memory with a cancellation process modelled on escaping a mobile phone contract.
