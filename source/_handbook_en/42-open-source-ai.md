If anyone can generate a contact application in an afternoon, why maintain an open source personal CRM?

Because an afternoon's implementation isn't the same thing as years of deciding what the application should mean, fixing its mistakes, and looking after the information people entrust to it.

The [reason we open source Monica](/en/handbook/why-open-source/) was never that our forms were impossible to reproduce.

### Understanding is still the scarce part

A generated feature can miss an assumption that a long-time maintainer recognizes immediately. Relationships have direction. Dates have context. A note about another person deserves care even if it is technically just text in a column.

Tools can help more people explore those problems and contribute solutions. That's exciting. They can also make it easier to submit a large change without understanding it, leaving the maintainer to do the difficult part.

For Monica, the useful contribution still includes an explanation of the problem, a change that fits the project, and someone willing to answer questions about it. The origin of the typing doesn't remove those needs.

Public code also remains useful for learning, inspection, and independence. Being able to generate new software doesn't give someone their existing memories back from an inaccessible service.

I expect the tools to change. I don't expect responsibility to disappear with the typing effort.

[Our engineering article](/en/handbook/ai-development/) describes the standard we want for assisted work. Faster production is welcome. Faster production of obligations nobody understands is less of a breakthrough.
