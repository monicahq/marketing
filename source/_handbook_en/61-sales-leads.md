Before building Monica, I tried using a professional CRM to remember things about friends and family. The [rebuild story](/en/blog/we-are-rebuilding-monica/) explains why that didn't fit very well.

A sales CRM has a commercial purpose. It helps organize work towards transactions. Nothing inherently wrong with that.

But a friendship doesn't have a closing date, an expected deal value, or a particularly sensible place in a conversion funnel.

### The category has limits

“Personal CRM” is a useful way to explain part of Monica quickly. It's also an invitation to import assumptions we don't want.

A note about a friend's child should help you remember a person, not identify an opportunity. A record of a conversation should preserve context, not become evidence that the relationship is underperforming.

That affects what we build. We shouldn't assign people a commercial value, rank friendships by activity, or suggest that a relationship has failed because the contact frequency looks low.

There can be structure without a performance target. A reminder can support an intention without turning someone into a task to complete.

[Not every relationship needs to be maintained](/en/handbook/letting-go/) takes this further: even the decision to stay in touch belongs to you.

I wanted a better memory, not a sales department for my personal life. The distinction is worth protecting, even if it makes the phrase “personal CRM” a little more complicated to explain.
