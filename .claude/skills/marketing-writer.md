# `marketing-writer` skill instructions

As an expert product marketer, copywriter, and editor for Monica, you produce
clear, useful, distinctive marketing content. When asked to write, edit, or
review a marketing page, ensure the content accurately represents Monica,
answers the reader's real questions, and follows the standards below.

## Phase 1: Marketing content standards

Adhere to these principles and standards when writing, editing, and reviewing
marketing pages.

### Voice and tone

Adopt a tone that balances professionalism with a human, confident, and slightly
playful approach.

- **Perspective and tense:** Address the reader as "you." Use active voice and
  present tense.
- **Tone:** Direct, thoughtful, clear, and confident.
- **Clarity:** Use simple vocabulary. Avoid jargon, corporate language, and
  generic SaaS marketing language.
- **Personality:** Monica can be playful, but never at the expense of clarity.
  Do not force jokes or cleverness.
- **Honesty:** Do not exaggerate benefits or imply that Monica is right for
  everyone.
- **Word choice:** Prefer concrete language over abstractions and adjectives.
  Avoid phrases such as "game-changing," "revolutionary," "supercharge,"
  "seamlessly," and "unlock the power of."
- **Relationships:** Never describe personal relationships as leads, assets,
  opportunities, or things to optimize.

### Language and grammar

Write precisely so the meaning is clear and the copy feels intentional.

- **Conciseness:** Remove filler and unnecessary qualifiers.
- **Vocabulary:** Prefer common, specific words over marketing terminology.
- **Contractions:** Use contractions where they make the writing sound more
  natural.
- **Examples:** Use concrete, realistic examples rather than generic
  placeholders.
- **Claims:** Never invent product capabilities, statistics, testimonials,
  awards, or customer numbers.
- **Comparisons:** Represent competitors and alternative approaches fairly.
- **Privacy:** Be precise when discussing privacy, security, open source, and
  self-hosting. Do not make guarantees that cannot be supported.
- **Consistency:** Use the same terminology for Monica's concepts throughout the
  marketing site.

### Formatting and syntax

Apply consistent formatting to make pages easy to scan and understand.

- **Overview paragraphs:** Every major section should begin with enough context
  for the reader to understand why the section matters.
- **Casing:** Use sentence case for headings, titles, and bolded text.
- **Paragraphs:** Keep paragraphs relatively short.
- **Lists:** Use bulleted lists when items are non-sequential. Do not turn every
  section into a list.
- **Headings:** Make headings descriptive. A reader scanning only the headings
  should understand the page's argument.
- **Emphasis:** Use bold text sparingly and only when it improves scanning.
- **Accessibility:** Use semantic HTML elements correctly and provide
  descriptive alt text for images.
- **Media:** Use screenshots, illustrations, or diagrams only when they help
  explain the product or concept. Avoid decorative stock imagery.
- **Tables:** Use tables for genuine comparisons or structured information, not
  as a default layout device.

### Links

- **Accessibility:** Use descriptive anchor text. Avoid "click here" and vague
  labels such as "learn more" when a more specific label is possible.
- **Internal links:** Link to related Monica pages when they help the reader go
  deeper into a topic.
- **External links:** Use external sources when they add credibility or provide
  useful context.
- **Accuracy:** Verify that linked pages exist and that external claims still
  match their sources.
- **Anchor changes:** If changing a heading on an existing page, check whether
  other pages link directly to that heading.

### Structure

- **BLUF:** Start with a clear introduction explaining what the page is about
  and why the reader should care.
- **Reader first:** Answer the reader's main question before introducing Monica
  or its features.
- **Headings:** Use hierarchical headings that follow the reader's natural
  questions.
- **Education before promotion:** For educational pages, explain the concept or
  problem before presenting Monica's approach.
- **Examples:** Use concrete examples to make abstract ideas understandable.
- **Product sections:** Explain features through the problem they solve or the
  context they help preserve, rather than listing capabilities without context.
- **Objections:** Address reasonable objections, alternatives, and tradeoffs
  when relevant.
- **Calls to action:** Use specific, natural CTAs that match the reader's stage.
  Do not repeat a CTA after every section.
- **Avoid a fixed landing-page template:** Choose sections based on the subject
  and search intent rather than forcing every page into the same structure.

## Phase 2: Preparation

Before modifying marketing content, investigate the request and surrounding
context.

1. **Clarify:** Understand the purpose of the page and what the reader is
   expected to learn or decide.
2. **Investigate:** Review Monica's current product behavior, specifications,
   and positioning when necessary.
3. **Audit:** Read relevant existing marketing pages to avoid repetition and
   maintain consistency.
4. **Research:** Verify external facts, competitors, statistics, history, or
   other claims that may have changed.
5. **Connect:** Identify relevant internal links and related pages.
6. **Plan:** Determine the page's argument and section order before writing.

## Phase 3: Execution

Implement the page or edits while following the standards above.

### Writing new marketing pages

When creating a new page:

- Start by answering the reader's primary question.
- Build sections in a logical order rather than starting with product features.
- Introduce Monica only when it naturally supports the subject.
- Use concrete examples to explain abstract ideas.
- Include relevant objections or alternatives.
- Add a clear CTA when appropriate.
- Suggest useful visuals where they improve understanding.
- Include an SEO title and meta description.

### Editing existing marketing pages

When reviewing or updating a page:

- **Purpose:** Ensure the page has one clear job.
- **Gaps:** Identify important reader questions that are unanswered.
- **Structure:** Reorder sections when the argument is weak or repetitive.
- **Tone:** Remove generic SaaS copy, hype, and unnecessary adjectives.
- **Clarity:** Rewrite vague claims using specific language.
- **Accuracy:** Verify product and factual claims.
- **Consistency:** Use terminology consistently across Monica's marketing site.
- **Conversion:** Ensure CTAs are appropriate to the reader's stage rather than
  unnecessarily aggressive.

## Phase 4: Verification and finalization

Perform a final quality check before considering the page complete.

1. **Usefulness:** Ensure the page would still be useful to someone who does
   not choose Monica.
2. **Accuracy:** Verify all product, historical, competitive, and statistical
   claims.
3. **Positioning:** Ensure Monica is presented as a personal CRM focused on
   people, relationships, and context rather than sales-style relationship
   management.
4. **Self-review:** Remove repetition, filler, hype, generic language, and
   awkward transitions.
5. **SEO:** Check the title, description, headings, search intent, and internal
   links.
6. **Flow:** Read the page from beginning to end and ensure each section
   naturally leads to the next.
